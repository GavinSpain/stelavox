5

If you think in structure, a blank page works against you.

And most AI tools go further — they write the book for you. Stelavox is built the other way around: a workspace shaped like your outline, where you hold the pen and the AI works only where you point it.

6
⌗

Structure-first writing

For writers who think in hierarchy, not a linear scroll. Your outline is a living tree — every layer a place to think, plan, and write. Rearrange freely; the structure holds.

◆

A Director, not an author

Propose, approve, done. The AI executes against your plan — nothing is written without your say-so.

🔒

Your work, yours alone

Encrypted at rest, private to your account. Export anytime in standard formats. No lock-in, ever.

Questions
Is my writing private?
Yes — encrypted at rest, private to your account, and exportable any time.
Does the AI write for me?
Only where you point it, and only after you approve its plan. You can also write everything yourself.
Do I have to outline everything before I start?
No. The tree supports structured thinking, but you can write at any layer, in any order, and grow the structure as you go.
Can I export and leave?
Always. Standard formats, no lock-in.

Soon — join the waitlist and you'll be first to know.

Annotations
1Nav wordmark — sized up. Bumped from a timid 15px to a confident 19px with a larger glowing lozenge, so the mark is “immediately recognizable” per header-logo guidance — quiet, not timid. Phased CTA: waitlist → open via marketing.signup_mode.
2Brand lockup + benefit headline. A confident wordmark lockup (30px Cinzel + Cormorant vox + glowing lozenge) gives the pre-launch, unknown brand a recall moment and uses the distinctive mark as the asset it is — but the benefit headline (Lora, largest element) still leads, per the research: message converts, brand signs. Eyebrow names the category; headline in the prose typeface ties the page to writing.
3Phased CTA — waitlist mode shown. Email + button → POST /api/waitlist. In open mode this becomes a single “Get started” button. Verdigris is the brand colour here (marketing surface — not bound by the app's 12-use rule).
4Hero visual. Speaks to the structured thinker — the tree front-and-centre, with the Director proposing a step. Two renderings compared in §02.
5The turn — both pains. Sentence 1 = the structured-thinker's frustration (blank linear page); sentence 2 = the authorship pain (AI writes for you); then the promise to both.
6Three value props (locked). Structure-first (leads — the distinctive hook) · Director-not-author (the trust pillar) · Your-work-yours.
7How it works. Four steps; step 4 reflects the new per-book export (DOCX/EPUB/Markdown).
8Pricing teaser (D2). One line → future /pricing. No full table on the landing page for V1.
9FAQ. Objection-handling incl. the new “do I have to outline everything?” (reassures discovery writers) + the waitlist “when does it open?”.
10·11D1 — hero two ways. A = stylised illustration (controllable, always on-brand). B = real app screenshot (more credible — shows real prose in Lora). Lean: B once the UI is settled; A as a fallback / for pre-screenshot launch.
12D4 — three tones. A quiet warm-paper (most literary, calmest). B confident dark (the §01 main — the app's signature surface). C warmer paper with a bolder, punchier headline (“doesn't write for you”) for more marketing pull. All within the brand system.
Decisions — your call from the mocks
D1 · Hero visual.Pick A or B Illustration (§02 left) vs real screenshot (§02 right). Recommend B once the live UI is camera-ready; A is the safe pre-screenshot option.
D4 · Tone.Pick A / B / C Quiet-paper vs confident-dark vs warmer-paper (§03). My read: B (confident dark) is the truest to the app and most distinctive; C if you want more overt marketing pull; A if you want maximum calm. Mixing is possible (e.g. C's punchier headline on B's dark surface).
