03 — Writer's eye · the first 10 seconds
A novelist just clicked "Sign in" for the first time

They've heard about Stelavox from a writer-friend or a podcast or a blog post. They had to create an account, verify an email, log in. Now they're here. They're skeptical — every AI writing tool to date has been some flavour of "generate slop" or "rewrite your sentences with a confident chirp." They're trying to see whether this is different. What does the page tell them in 10 seconds?

What this version says

The Wordmark + tagline says "this is a craft tool, not a productivity tool". The italic Cormorant tagline lands. The three-line philosophy strip at the bottom says "structured / directed / quiet" — three words that distinguish this from a chat-with-your-novel app. The two CTAs respect the writer's choice: "I'll commit to my own work" or "let me see what this does first."

The sidebar is still present but hinted-empty — the writer can see the shape of the space they'll fill, which is more honest than hiding the chrome and pretending nothing exists yet.

What I'd want to push further or revise
F-1
"Tour with a sample novel" is the unknown variable. The wireframe shows the button but the actual tour experience hasn't been designed. The right shape is probably: clicking this copies Shadow Protocol (or a subset) into the user's account as a real project they can edit, with a small banner saying "This is a sample. Feel free to delete, modify, or use it to learn." That's better than a guided walkthrough because writers learn by doing, not by reading callouts. Open question: is "Shadow Protocol" the right sample, or do we author a deliberately smaller one (single chapter, ~10 beats) called "The Lighthouse" or similar?
F-2
The welcome copy might be too long. Four sentences with three pieces of jargon (tree, Director, Focus Mode) before the writer has done anything. Consider a tighter version: "A writing environment built around structure, AI assistance, and quiet focus." One line, no jargon. The three-line strip below already names the concepts. Less text, more breathing room.
F-3
The "Read the introduction" link is undefined. Either we have introduction content (Phase 12 — User documentation) or we don't. If we don't have it by V1 launch, the link shouldn't appear. Recommend dropping it for now; replace with "or see how it works" linking to a 60-second video later if/when we make one.
F-4
"+ Create your first project" is the right CTA but the next click is undesigned. Probably a modal asking for project name + document type (Novel / Thesis / Paper / Custom) + first-document name. Currently undesigned — flag for the project-creation modal wireframe (8.01 secondary, or own wireframe).
F-5
Two CTAs side-by-side is the right pattern for V1. Avoid stacking three or four — the moment you offer four paths the writer feels they have to choose correctly. Two paths = "real work" or "look around." That's the right amount of choice on first-run.
F-6
The empty sidebar hint "Your projects will appear here" could be more inviting. Try "You'll see your projects here." — direct second-person address, like Stelavox is talking to the writer. Or even: "Your novel is going to live here." Slightly bolder. Worth a sub-iteration.
F-7
No mention of "your work is yours." A writer signing up has implicit anxiety about AI tools and their work. A single line, somewhere quiet, that says "your prose is yours. We don't train on your work." would carry a lot of weight. Could live in the foot row or as a fourth tile in the philosophy strip. Recommend addition.
F-8
The page is large; ~820px tall. Below the fold (which on a laptop is around 700–750px) currently sits the three-line philosophy strip. Some users won't scroll. Either compress the hero vertically so the strip is in-view at standard laptop heights, or accept that the strip is "for those who scroll a little." I'd compress.
Honest gut feel

The first-time page is meaningfully better than what would have been there (i.e., a generic auth-flow landing or a confusing dashboard with one "+ New" button). The Wordmark + tagline + three philosophy lines do real work in 10 seconds. The two-path CTA respects the writer's caution. The single line I'd most want to add is F-7 — "your work is yours" — because it answers an unstated question that's true for every writer trying an AI tool. Beyond that, F-1 (sample tour) is the most consequential UX choice we haven't fully resolved: is it a real-data copy, or a guided overlay, or both?

Current: 4 sentences with 3 pieces of jargon. Tighter alternative: 1 sentence, defer concepts to the philosophy strip. Try in iter4?
F-3 introduction link
