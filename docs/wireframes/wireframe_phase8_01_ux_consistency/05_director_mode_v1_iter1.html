What makes this Director Mode feel different
1
Header has presence. Stelavox "S" mark in a verdigris gradient at 32px — gives the Director an identity rather than just a "Director Panel" label. Status line tells you exactly what's happening ("Working · ~2 min remaining"). Stop button always visible when active.
2
User message in Inter, surfaced as a card. Your messages have visual weight — they're clearly demarcated from the Director's replies. Bracketed-label mentions ([Sc 1] The Iron Ghost) render as monospace verdigris chips. Same syntax everywhere.
3
"Reasoning · 6 lines" chip replaces visible <plan> tags. The Director's internal planning is hidden by default but you can click to expand. You don't have to read the model's thinking-aloud unless you want to. Conversation reads as conversation.
4
Tool calls as compact chips. "read [Sc 1] The Iron Ghost · read prose · 3 beats" — shows what the Director looked at without dominating the message. Clickable to see results if you want. Same monospace family as labels everywhere.
5
Director response body in Lora 14.5px. The Director is a writer talking to a writer; prose typography signals that. Bold names of beats/scenes/chapters break out structurally so they're scannable. The whole thing reads like a craft conversation, not a chat-with-an-LLM.
6
Tree pane stays visible as context. Compressed to 340px to make room for the Director panel. Mentioned nodes get a verdigris left-border + @ prefix marker in the tree — visual coupling between conversation and structure. You always see where in the tree the conversation is anchored.
7
Workflow proposal as a distinct card. Verdigris border (only place verdigris fills a border — it signals "actionable proposal"). Tag, title, meta line (steps · time · cost), per-step description with mention chips, big Approve / Reject buttons. Approve · Run sends it to the scheduler immediately. The proposal is clearly inline in the conversation thread, not a separate modal.

04 — Writer's eye · the Director in a real exchange
The Director as collaborator, not chat-with-an-LLM

Most AI tools I've used as a writer feel like they're showing off — long-winded responses, visible reasoning blocks, sycophantic preambles, code-block-ish output where prose would do. The bar for "feels like talking to a craft collaborator" is honestly quite low because almost no one clears it. This iteration is the first time I've thought "oh — this is what the Director should feel like."

What works in iter1

The Lora 14.5px body text on the Director's responses is doing more emotional work than I expected. Prose-typography signals "this is a writer talking to a writer." Compare to standard chat-UI sans-serif at 14px — the same content reads completely differently. Visual register matters when the topic is craft.

Bracketed mentions threaded through the prose make the structural references unambiguous without breaking the conversational tone. "The pacing in [Sc 1] The Iron Ghost works in beats 1 and 3 but breaks in [Bt 2] Recognition" — both completely natural English AND completely precise. Same vocabulary the writer uses in their head.

The Reasoning chip is the single biggest UX win. The model can think aloud in <plan> tags all it wants — the writer never has to see it. One subtle pill in the corner; click if you're debugging, ignore otherwise. This is the right resolution to the "Director output is verbose XML" problem we surfaced earlier.

The proposal card lands inline with the conversation — not as a separate modal, not as a sidebar, not as a "click here to see the proposal." The conversation produces the proposal as its natural next utterance, and the Approve/Reject buttons are right there. Frictionless. This is how I'd want to direct an assistant.

What I'd push or revise
M-1
The Director's mark / avatar. Currently an "S" in a verdigris gradient — I went with "S" for Stelavox but on reflection the Director isn't really Stelavox-the-tool — it's the agent the tool gives you access to. Worth considering: a single brand glyph (◈ from the project lozenge?), or an abstract mark like ◇, or even just the Wordmark "S" in Cinzel. The current choice is fine but worth A/B'ing.
M-2
"You" and "The Director" as headers above each message. Standard chat-UI affordance but maybe overkill in a 2-party conversation. Alternative: drop the headers and rely on visual styling alone (user = surface card, Director = Lora-bodied open prose). Cleaner; less repetitive. Tradeoff: less scannable when reviewing old conversations. I lean keep-for-now, revisit when we have real conversation depth.
M-3
Time stamps as "4 min ago" / "3 min ago". Relative time is friendly but for an exchange that just happened it adds noise. Counter: hide the timestamp on the most recent message; show on older ones. Or show only on hover. Quiet by default.
M-4
"Working · ~2 min remaining" estimate. If we can't be reasonably accurate the estimate becomes annoying. Better might be "Working on it · Stop" with no time estimate, or an honest "First step running · 3 steps total" progress. Estimates are a promise; we shouldn't make ones we can't keep.
M-5
Mentioned-node highlighting in the tree pane. Currently the mentioned beat ([Bt 2] Recognition) gets a verdigris left-border + @ prefix in the tree. Conceptually right — visual coupling between conversation and structure. But it might be visually noisy if the conversation references 10+ nodes. Limit: highlight only the most recently-mentioned 3 nodes; let earlier mentions fade.
M-6
Tree pane width. 340px compresses the tree but row text starts truncating with longer node names. Worth testing: should the Director panel become a slide-over on narrower screens (iPad), with the tree at full width when no Director is open and the panel sliding in over the top half of the tree? Defer to T-8 iPad wireframe.
M-7
"Reasoning · 6 lines" — should the lines count refer to the raw reasoning chars, or the conceptual steps? A 6-line reasoning block could be a brief plan or a dense analysis. Better label might be just "Reasoning ▸" without the line count — opens to show whatever's there. Counter: line count gives you a sense of "how much thinking did the Director do."
M-8
"Approve · Run" vs separate Approve and Run. Currently one button approves AND immediately dispatches. Some authors might want "Approve" (saves the workflow as planned) separately from "Run" (dispatches it). For V1 I think combined is fine — the friction of a two-step is not worth the safety of separating. Flag for V2.
Honest gut feel

This is the wireframe I've been most excited to draft. The Director Mode has been the bit that most differentiated Stelavox from generic AI writing tools, and the previous implementation buried the magic under XML tags and same-as-edit-mode chrome. Iter1 here lets the conversation be the conversation. The two real next steps are M-4 (honest progress display) and the eventual iPad layout (M-6 → T-8 wireframe).
